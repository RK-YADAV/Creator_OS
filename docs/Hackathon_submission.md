01
Why this exists
We have watched many cohorts reach this exact week. Lectures end, the capstone begins, and most teams spend the first fortnight reading, planning and choosing tools. By the time they talk to a real person, it is too late to change anything.

The first action is always the same, and it is never technical: find the people your capstone is for, learn how they do the work today, and do that work for them once, by hand. If you cannot convert their input into a useful output yourself, you cannot automate it, and you should not build an agent for it.

This hackathon exists to make that first action happen this weekend instead of in October. Your capstone deadline is 7 October. Offline Demo Day in Bengaluru is 11 October. Everything you produce in these 48 hours is the foundation of what you present there.

How Siddhant did it with Sahaj Fresh
He told the client he would not sell them software. He read their data, formed a judgment by hand about what would help, and delivered that judgment to them directly. Only once the process was known did he add a thin deterministic layer to do his own part faster. Manual for them, automated for him, and the automation came second.

02
The one rule
Manual for the user. Any tool for you.
You may use any AI model, skill, script or tool to do your own work: research, interview prep, drafting, checking, note-taking.

You may not hand the user an app, an agent, a bot or a system. What reaches them is something you produced, by hand, from their real input.

The distinction matters because the thing you are proving this weekend is the process: the steps that turn this person's input into an output they find useful. Software built before the process is known is decoration. Software built after it is the capstone.

Only existing capstone teams take part. No new teams form for this hackathon. Your submission is one folder per team.

03
What you submit: one folder, ten artefacts
Completion is binary. All ten present means complete. Nine present means not complete, and judging only begins for complete folders. Each artefact is a short document, a screenshot or a file inside your team's folder. Length is not credit.

1
Observation
One paragraph. Who does the work, what they do, where it breaks. No solution words. You have written it properly when a stranger can retell it without your phrasing.
2
Impact metric
One number with a unit, and how you would measure it before and after. Passes: "from 40 minutes per report to under 10". Fails: "saves time".
3
Hypothesis
Your capstone hypothesis rewritten in your own words, with a number and one kill condition: the result that would tell you it is wrong.
4
Two named users
Name, role, and how you reached them. Not you and not your own team members. A classmate, friend or family member counts if they genuinely match your brief’s user. If your brief needs a specific kind of person (a creator with 20 pieces, a learner, someone holding a costly belief), that is who they must be.
5
Contact artefact, one per user no notes without this
A chat or email screenshot showing the conversation and a timestamp. Blur or crop anything sensitive. Notes without a contact artefact count as zero users.
6
Interview notes, one per user
What they told you about how they do the work today, and the real input they gave you to work on.
7
The manual run
The actual output you handed each user, produced from their real input, by you. Attach it as they received it.
8
User reaction, in their words
One specific sentence from each user about what you gave them. "Nice" is not a reaction. "The second point is wrong, we stopped doing that in June" is.
9
Process map
The steps you actually took from input to output, in order. Colour each step D (a rule, a lookup, arithmetic), P (a judgment call) or H (a human had to be in the room). This is your system design. It is what you build from.
10
What broke
One line naming where the manual process was slowest, hardest or wrong. That is the first thing you automate, and the first thing you defend at Demo Day.

05
How to start in the next hour
Reread your brief's Observation once, then close it. Write artefact 1 in your own words before you write anything else.
List ten people who match your brief's user. Not "operations people". Names, or at least a place where those names live: a Discord server, a WhatsApp group, a LinkedIn search, a family member's colleague.
Send the first message tonight, in your own words. Screenshot the send. That is artefact 5 starting.
Split the team. One person owns the users, one owns the manual run, one owns the folder. Every member must appear in at least one contact artefact or interview.
Do not open a code editor. Open a doc. If you have used the discovery-to-mvp-coach skill before, it will get you through artefacts 1 to 3 quickly. The skill is allowed. The skill's output is not the submission; the users are.
06
Your first action, by brief
Every brief has the same shape this weekend: one user type, one manual output, one number. Custom capstones follow the same ten artefacts with the user and metric from your own validated brief.

Genome Intelligence
Users
Two people who have a raw genotype file (23andMe, AncestryDNA or similar) and are willing to share it with you privately.
Manual run
Pick 3 to 5 variants in one domain by hand. For each, write one card: what supports it, what disputes it, how old the evidence is, which population it was measured in. Hand over the cards, not a report.
Number
How many of your findings the user challenged or rejected after reading the evidence. That share is your signal.
Creator OS
Users
Two creators or small content teams with at least 20 published pieces you can read.
Manual run
Take one piece of raw material from them (a recording, a call, a voice note). Draft one publishable output for a channel they use. Before handing it over, check every claim against the source and against their archive yourself, and mark what you checked.
Number
Minutes they spent rewriting before they would publish it, and whether it was accepted, fixed, or abandoned.
Collaborative Intelligence
Users
Two people outside your team who do one repeated task often enough to see it several times this weekend.
Manual run
Watch them do the task twice and write down what they contribute that a prompt could not. Then do the task yourself with a plain model prompt, no system. Record both results side by side. That is arms one and two of your three-arm comparison, before any build.
Number
Time and quality per run for the person alone and for the bare model. Your pair has to beat both later.
Learning OS
Users
Two learners outside your team who can run SQL against the given schema.
Manual run
Give each a plain English question. Coach them over chat by hand, giving hints and never the answer, until their result set is correct. The next day, give a held-out question with no help. Record whether the result set was correct.
Number
Unaided correctness on the held-out question. Also record the exact hints you gave: that log is your policy about how much to give.
Verified Persuasion
Users
Two people who each hold one belief that costs them money, time or health, can say why they hold it, and consent to the conversation. Political and identity beliefs are out.
Manual run
Ask why they hold it. Write five claims that answer their reason, each traceable to a source you name. Talk them through it. Rate the belief 0 to 10 before and after.
Number
Belief shift, and a count of claims you made that you could not source. That second number should be zero.
Custom capstone
Users
The user your validated brief names. If your brief could not name one, this weekend is when you find out.
Manual run
One input from a real user, one output produced by you, delivered to them.
Number
The impact metric from your brief, measured once by hand.
07
How it is judged
Stage 1: complete or not
The 100x Program team checks every folder for the ten artefacts. Present or absent, no judgment. Incomplete folders stop here.

Stage 2: four criteria, read by Siddhant
Each criterion scores 0, 1 or 2. The criteria are written so two people reading the same folder would agree.

Criterion	2 points	0 points
Specificity	Users are named people. The metric has a digit and a unit. The bottleneck in artefact 10 is one step.	"The sales team", "saves time", "it is slow".
Reality	Contact artefacts are real and timestamped. The manual output was produced from input the user actually gave.	Invented input, or notes with no evidence of contact.
Reaction quality	Each user said something specific enough to change your design.	"Looks good", "interesting", or paraphrased by the team.
Process honesty	The D/P/H map matches what actually happened and names where it failed.	A map of what you plan to build, or a map with no failure named.
Tie break: the team whose first contact artefact carries the earliest timestamp after kickoff.

Complexity is not credit
Nothing in the rubric rewards tools used, models called, or components planned. Two real people, one honest manual run and a process map you can defend beat any amount of architecture.

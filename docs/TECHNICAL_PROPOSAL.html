I-PostPilot | Technical Proposal | September 2026

Multimodal Clip Intelligence

A performance-oriented technical architecture for converting long-form creator video into timestamp-accurate, creator-aligned, platform-ready clip proposals. The system uses a cascade of deterministic processing, multimodal candidate ranking, grounded generation, and feedback-driven personalization.

1. Executive decision

Recommendation: Do not build an embedding-only or rules-only detector. Build a cascade that generates a high-recall set of possible moments, aligns them to transcript/audio/video boundaries, ranks them with creator-specific evidence, and uses a strong multimodal model only for the top candidates.

The central product value is not “finding semantic changes.” It is reducing the time and effort required to turn a long-form recording into clips the creator would actually publish. Embeddings, rules, audio features, vision, and LLMs are therefore features in a ranking system, not competing product strategies.

Recall first
Generate enough candidate moments before ranking.
Multimodal
Text alone misses pauses, energy, visuals, and demonstrations.
Personalized
Approved and rejected examples define creator fit.
Measured
Compare against manual and simpler baselines.

2. Product hypothesis and falsification criteria

The technical system should test this falsifiable claim:

If I-PostPilot uses a creator's archive, multimodal content signals, and persistent feedback to rank clip candidates, then creators will reach publishable clips faster than their normal manual workflow without unacceptable loss of quality or voice.

What would disprove it?

  • Time-to-publish is not materially lower than manual selection.
  • Creators make major edits to most proposals.
  • Timestamp corrections are frequent enough to remove the time benefit.
  • Creator-specific archive and feedback data do not improve ranking over a generic baseline.
  • Creators do not want to reuse the workflow after testing it.
  • High-ranked candidates are not more likely to be accepted than lower-ranked candidates.

3. Target system architecture

The architecture separates media truth, AI interpretation, ranking, and human decisions. Every generated proposal must retain evidence back to source media and transcript words.

deterministic/media AI intelligence persistence human review

4. End-to-end processing pipeline

01 / INGESTNormalize media

FFmpeg, ffprobe, loudness normalization, proxy generation, duration and codec validation.

02 / SPEECHBuild speech timeline

Whisper large-v3 or managed ASR, word timestamps, speaker diarization, punctuation, confidence.

03 / AUDIOExtract prosody

VAD, pauses, energy, pitch, speech rate, laughter, applause, and interruption signals.

04 / VIDEOBuild visual timeline

Shot boundaries, keyframes, OCR, slide/screen detection, faces, framing, and crop safety.

05 / RETRIEVEUnderstand creator

Hybrid search over individual archive examples, approvals, rejections, metadata, and performance.

06 / GENERATECreate candidates

Generate 15, 30, 45, 60, and 90 second windows aligned to words, pauses, sentences, and shots.

07 / RANKPersonalize ranking

Transparent features first, then learning-to-rank and cross-encoder reranking as labels accumulate.

08 / REVIEWGrounded proposals

Multimodal LLM reviews top candidates, creates copy, validates evidence, and presents playback for approval.

Candidate scoring principle

score =
    narrative_score
  + creator_match_score
  + self_containedness
  + audio_energy_score
  + visual_context_score
  + semantic_novelty
  - context_dependency_penalty
  - boundary_penalty
  - transcript_uncertainty_penalty

The score is initially transparent and inspectable. After enough creator decisions, replace or augment the fixed weights with a learning-to-rank model. Do not expose this raw score as a probability called “confidence” until it has been calibrated.

6. Evidence and data model

The system must preserve a complete evidence graph. A proposal should be traceable from generated title and captions back to candidate window, transcript words, audio/visual events, archive examples, ranking features, and creator decision. 

Creator
  -> ArchiveItem
      -> transcript, metadata, metrics, approved/rejected labels
      -> embeddings and topic/style annotations
  -> VideoAsset
      -> word timestamps, speakers, shots, keyframes, audio features
  -> CandidateWindow
      -> start/end, transcript word IDs, feature vector, ranking scores
  -> Proposal
      -> generated copy, evidence references, model/prompt version
  -> CreatorDecision
      -> action, reason, edits, time spent, final boundary

Minimum archive inputs

Content

  • 10-30 historical videos or transcripts
  • Published short clips and source ranges
  • Titles, descriptions, dates, platforms

Performance

  • Watch time and completion rate
  • Shares, saves, comments, follows
  • Audience size at publication

Preferences

  • Preferred duration and tone
  • Words/topics to avoid
  • Clickbait and context tolerance

Negative evidence

  • Rejected candidates and reasons
  • Major boundary edits
  • “Good for long-form” labels

7. Evaluation design

Model quality must be evaluated against workflows, not only similarity thresholds. Use a fixed holdout set and compare the complete pipeline against strong, understandable baselines.

  1. Manual baseline: creator selects clips using the normal workflow.
  2. Transcript baseline: sentence/keyword/rule candidates with manual review.
  3. Embedding baseline: semantic retrieval and topic-change candidates.
  4. Hybrid ranker: text, audio, visual, archive, and boundary features.
  5. Full system: hybrid ranker plus multimodal LLM reranking and grounded copy.
Evaluation layerMeasuresMinimum evidence
Boundary qualityStart/end correction rate, word alignment, context dependency.Creator review plus sampled manual annotation.
Ranking qualityPrecision@K, recall@K, NDCG, pairwise preference accuracy.Accepted/rejected candidate labels.
Generation qualityCaption correction, unsupported claim rate, style match.Schema checks and creator edits.
Workflow valueTime-to-first-clip, time-to-five-clips, review burden.Timed sessions across creators and videos.
Business signalPublished clips, retention, reuse intent.Post-publication metrics where available.
Do not claim “90% accuracy” for questions, audio energy, archive keywords, or fine-tuning without a labeled test set. Precision is a measured result, not a property of a heuristic.

10. Final recommendation

Build the evaluation-ready cascade first. The winning implementation is not the one with the largest model. It is the one that produces accurate boundaries, finds complete and publishable moments, adapts to each creator, explains its evidence, and demonstrably reduces time-to-publish against a manual baseline.
Near term: transcript + audio + archive retrieval + transparent hybrid ranking.
Next: persistent feedback and learning-to-rank from creator preferences.
Then: visual intelligence and multimodal reranking for the top candidates.
Later: rendering, publishing integrations, shared models, and creator-specific adapters.
